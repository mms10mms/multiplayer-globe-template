From the editor

My goal is to keep you up to date on the cyber world, and to build a repository of everything that happens in it. Every day I gather the newest vulnerabilities, active attacks, breaches and malware from trusted sources and publish short, plain-language summaries you can read in minutes.

Threats change daily, and most people do not have time to follow them. I do that reading for you. Each item links back to where it came from, and I read every item before it goes live. No single source catches everything, so the repository is something I am working toward, not something that is finished.

“Plans are nothing, planning is everything.”

Dwight D. Eisenhower

Cybersecurity changes every day. A plan that is airtight today can leave you back at square one tomorrow. That is why the planning matters more than any single plan: stay ahead of the threats, and prepare for every outcome you can.

How every item is made

Software gathers the news. A person decides what is accurate and worth your time.

  1. 1. CollectSoftware pulls new stories every day from trusted security news and vendor sources.
  2. 2. SummarizeAI drafts a short summary of what happened and who is affected, using only what the source says.
  3. 3. ReviewI read every item, fix it or reject it, and approve only what is accurate.
  4. 4. PublishApproved items go up in the feeds with a link to the source, newest first.

Coming up

What you see today is the start. Here is what I am building next, and I will share what I learn as I go.

Automated CTI pipelines

CTI is cyber threat intelligence. I am building pipelines in my homelab that keep threat tracking running around the clock, for my own research and write-ups. The goal is not just to collect threats, but to understand the reasoning behind them: why an attack works, what made a target vulnerable, and how a campaign connects to others.

An AI-assisted SIEM experiment

A SIEM is a tool that collects security logs and flags suspicious activity. I want to experiment with building an automated, or at least assisted, SIEM that uses AI agents to help sort alerts, spot patterns and explain what they found. This is an experiment, not a product.

Section · Malware

Malware analysis

What malicious software does, how it spreads and how to spot it: worms, ransomware, infostealers and more, explained in plain language.

What this section covers

Ransomware

Malware that locks or steals data and demands payment, and the groups that run it.

Worms

Malware that spreads from system to system on its own, with no one clicking anything.

Infostealers

Malware that takes passwords, browser data, keys and tokens, which are then sold or reused.

Trojans and loaders

Programs that look harmless, or quietly download something worse once inside.

Botnets

Networks of infected devices controlled by an attacker, used for attacks and spam.

Supply chain malware

Malicious code hidden in trusted software, updates and developer tools.

CTI pipeline

Cyber threat intelligence (CTI) is the work of collecting data about threats and turning it into something useful. This is my own CTI project, built in my homelab for research. It is still in progress.

What it is for

  • Collecting threat data in one place, including what my honeypots see.
  • Keeping threat tracking running all the time, not just once a day.
  • Going beyond what happened to understand why: why an attack works, what made a target vulnerable, and how campaigns connect.
  • Feeding my own research and write-ups.

What it is not

It does not produce the stories on this site. The Threat Summary, AI Research and Malware feeds come from a separate news collector: software gathers articles from trusted security sources, AI drafts a short summary, and I review every item before it is published. The CTI pipeline is a research project that runs alongside that, in the lab.

Where it stands

Being built. Parts of it run in my homelab, which is not finished yet. I will post what it does and what I learn here as it comes together.
