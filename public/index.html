Coming soon

What you see today is the start. Here is what we are building next, and we will share what we learn as we go.

Automated CTI pipelines

CTI is cyber threat intelligence. We are building automated pipelines that watch for new threats around the clock, so our feeds can keep up with attackers instead of waiting for the next daily roundup. The goal is not just to collect threats, but to understand the reasoning behind them: why an attack works, what made a target vulnerable, and how a campaign connects to others. Knowing the why is what separates useful analysis from a list of headlines.

An AI-assisted SIEM experiment

A SIEM is a tool that collects security logs and flags suspicious activity. We want to experiment with building an automated, or at least assisted, SIEM that uses AI agents to help sort alerts, spot patterns and explain what they found. This is an experiment, not a product. We will test what works and what does not, and share the results.

Malware analysis

What malicious software does, how it spreads and how to spot it. We follow worms, ransomware, infostealers and other malware, and explain each in plain language.

Ransomware

Malware that locks or steals data and demands payment, and the groups that run it.

Worms

Malware that spreads from system to system on its own, with no one clicking anything.

Infostealers

Malware that takes passwords, browser data, keys and tokens, which are then sold or reused.

Trojans and loaders

Programs that look harmless, or quietly download something worse once inside.

Botnets

Networks of infected devices controlled by an attacker, used for attacks and spam.

Supply chain malware

Malicious code hidden in trusted software, updates and developer tools.
